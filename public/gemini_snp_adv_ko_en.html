Professional Comprehensive Analysis: Caution Amidst Overheating: What the Data Reveals About the Market's Undercurrents

The current S&P500 Fear & Greed Index stands at 64.97, positioning it within the 'Greed' zone. This indicates that optimistic investor sentiment is driving the market, while the VIX index, also maintaining a low level at 14.63, suggests minimal short-term volatility concerns. However, it is crucial to thoroughly examine what risk factors might be hidden beneath this optimism.

  • Sustained Tight Monetary Policy: As of July 1, 2026, the U.S. federal funds rate remains elevated at 3.63%, acting as a restrictive pressure on the market. Although there are expectations that inflation has somewhat stabilized, this interest rate level continues to influence corporate borrowing costs and investment decisions, serving as a factor to curb indiscriminate overheating.
  • Robust but Cooling Job Market: As of July 1, 2026, non-farm payrolls increased by 158,858, and the unemployment rate stood at 4.1%. While these figures still indicate a robust labor market, if the pace of increase slows or the unemployment rate slightly rises compared to previous periods, this could be interpreted as a 'hard landing concern' rather than 'delayed rate cut concerns.' In the current 'greed' market, strong employment does not heighten expectations for rate cuts, thereby serving to temper excessive market optimism.
  • Comparison with Past Greed Phases: The current Fear & Greed Index in the mid-60s bears resemblance to the euphoric atmosphere just before the dot-com bubble burst in the early 2000s. At that time, the VIX also remained low, and a strong technology-driven rally unfolded. While the macroeconomic environment is different, historical precedents remind us that excessive optimism can always lead to a correction. Especially when market concentration in specific sectors intensifies as it does now, investors need to adopt a more cautious approach.

Prudent Advice: The Wisdom of Savvy Investors: A Cool-headed Exit Strategy Amidst Greed

First, Profit Realization and Portfolio Rebalancing

In the current 'Greed' phase, it is advisable to be wary of market overheating and consider realizing some profits from assets that have already yielded significant returns. This is a wise strategy to prepare for potential corrections and diversify risk through portfolio rebalancing. Specifically, it is recommended to gradually reduce the proportion of high-volatility assets and diversify into assets that generate stable cash flows.

Second, Thorough Corporate Valuation Analysis

When the market shows strength, 'blind investing' not based on fundamentals tends to become prevalent. Therefore, it is essential to thoroughly analyze the financial health, growth potential, and competitive advantages of the companies you intend to invest in, avoiding stocks that are overvalued relative to their intrinsic worth. Focusing on solid companies from a long-term perspective is crucial.

Third, Secure Cash Reserves in Preparation for Increased Volatility

A low VIX index does not always signify a calm market. Volatility can increase at any time due to unforeseen macroeconomic events or deteriorating corporate earnings. By securing a certain level of cash in preparation for such situations, you can seize buying opportunities during market corrections or mitigate portfolio shocks. 'Ammunition' should always be ready.
